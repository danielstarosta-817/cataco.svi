# Master candidate index

Every case currently in the tool (live and dimmed) plus every candidate raised across roughly twenty research passes, in one place. Organized by category, ranked loosely by strength within each (strongest first), tagged by status. This is a pruning document, not a finished structure, the category groupings below mostly mirror how the research was actually conducted (place-by-place, then theme-by-theme), not necessarily how the tool should ultimately group things. A few corrections surfaced while assembling this that are worth flagging up front: San Francisco 1906 and the Netherlands are already dimmed cases in the tool, not new candidates, an earlier document mistakenly flagged both as gaps. Lisbon is already a full live case, not a candidate, though the new vernacular-architecture research substantially deepens it.

Status tags: **LIVE** (full case in the tool), **DIMMED** (thin/unresearched stub in the tool), **NEW** (not in the tool at all).

## C. New geographic candidates (whole places, not yet touched)

Ranked roughly by how complete and citable the existing case for each already is.

**Exceptional**
- **Assam, India — the Brahmaputra's flood cycle and Ambubachi Mela.** Promoted from "thin" after a deeper pass. Ambubachi Mela at Guwahati's Kamakhya Temple is a major religious festival whose calendar is explicitly locked to the river's monsoon flood state (held each June when the Brahmaputra is "in spate"), not a harvest or lunar date detached from the water; a nearby spring feeding the river runs reddish for the festival's three days, causation genuinely disputed (iron oxide, cinnabar, algae, or votive vermilion, no scientific consensus), same honest-ambiguity treatment as the Tlaloc entry elsewhere. Paired with Majuli Island's Vaishnavite satras, centuries-old monasteries and the region's living centers for classical dance and mask-making, down from 65 to roughly 22 as erosion forces relocations, projected to lose the whole island within two decades. Full detail in its own document.
- **Dust Bowl, US Great Plains, 1930s.** Guthrie's *Dust Bowl Ballads*, Steinbeck's *Grapes of Wrath*, Lange's "Migrant Mother," Rothstein's "Fleeing a Dust Storm." Probably the single most culturally over-documented American disaster not yet in the tool.
- **Pompeii and Herculaneum, Italy — 79 CE.** Pliny the Younger's letters (first eyewitness disaster account in Western literature), the Pompeii body casts, 2,000 years of continuous cultural output, and Pliny the Elder is already the tool's own Formulation epigraph.
- **Mount Tambora, 1815 / "Year Without a Summer," 1816.** Directly produced *Frankenstein* and Byron's "Darkness" in the same rented villa, the same month. Exceptionally clean, exceptionally famous.
- **Armero, Colombia — 1985 Nevado del Ruiz lahar.** The Omayra Sánchez photograph is one of the most reproduced disaster images in the world; strong Colombian musical response too.

**Strong**
- **Galveston, Texas — 1900 hurricane.** Deadliest US natural disaster by death toll; "Wasn't That a Mighty Storm" has a clean century-plus chain of transmission (1900s spiritual → 1934 Library of Congress recording → 1960s folk revival → James Taylor, 2009).
- **Bhola cyclone, Bangladesh — 1970.** Deadliest tropical cyclone on record; inspired the 1971 Concert for Bangladesh and led directly to Bangladesh's cyclone shelter/early-warning network — a rare direct line from a benefit song to real infrastructure.
- **Johnstown, Pennsylvania — 1889 flood.** Sheet music copyrighted the same year, a dedicated flood museum with a standing music gallery, and the actual dam-failure site preserved as the memorial itself.
- **Tangshan, China — 1976 earthquake.** 240,000+ dead, actively suppressed under Mao, resurfaced decades later via the blockbuster *Aftershock* (2010) — a clean Degradation-then-revival arc.
- **Ashgabat, Turkmenistan — 1948 earthquake.** Death toll classified for 40 years under Soviet rule; about as clean a state-suppression case as exists.
- **Halifax, Nova Scotia — 1917 explosion.** The annual Boston Christmas tree (sent nearly every year since 1918) plus Solnit's mutual-aid material plus the Fort Needham bell tower memorial — three separate strong threads already researched.
- **Yungay, Peru — 1970 avalanche.** An entire town of ~20,000 buried in seconds; the site left undisturbed as a memorial rather than rebuilt over.
- **Mount Merapi, Java, Indonesia.** The *juru kunci* spiritual-gatekeeper tradition, and the 2010 case where the gatekeeper died trusting tradition over a government evacuation order — a genuinely hard Operationalizing-vs-tradition tension.

**Solid**
- Sri Lanka — 2004 tsunami (the derailed passenger train, now an informal pilgrimage site).
- Mount Pinatubo, Philippines — 1991 (Aeta displacement and oral tradition; pairs with the already-dimmed Krakatoa).
- Bhuj/Kutch, Gujarat, India — 2001 earthquake (embroidery-economy revival, a rare upbeat Operationalizing story).
- Nepal — 2015 Gorkha earthquake (Newar temple-carpenter apprentice tradition).
- Messina, Italy — 1908 earthquake (deadliest in European history, but outshined by Lisbon/Pompeii in memory).
- Skopje, North Macedonia — 1963 earthquake (Cold War joint-reconstruction aid, later "Skopje 2014" architectural overwrite).
- Aberfan, Wales — 1966 (116 children killed; raw, present, recently reintroduced via *The Crown*).
- Laki eruption, Iceland — 1783–84 (killed ~20–25% of Iceland's population; also flagged independently in the Lucy Jones document).
- Venice, Italy — Acqua Alta / MOSE barriers (pairs naturally with the Netherlands case).
- Ireland — the Great Famine, 1845–52 (diaspora-sustained memory rather than place-sustained).
- Sahel drought/famine cycles, West Africa (griot oral tradition — now substantially deepened by the Africa and oral-tradition documents below).
- Goma, DRC — Mount Nyiragongo (1977/2002/2021) (a city repeatedly rebuilt on its own lava).
- Valdivia, Chile — 1960 (largest earthquake ever recorded; needs a dedicated cultural-response search before committing).
- Managua, Nicaragua — 1972 earthquake (corruption in aid response credited as a spark for the Sandinista revolution).

## D. Deep-time deities and mythic carvings

**Exceptional**
- The Gilgamesh flood tablet, Mesopotamia (7th c. BCE copy of a much older story) — arguably the tool's own founding artifact for Formulation itself; construction specs for Utnapishtim's boat embedded directly in the text.
- The Aztec Sun Stone (Piedra del Sol) — Five Suns cosmology, catastrophe as the basic structure of cosmic time.
- Chaac masks, Governor's Palace, Uxmal — 200+ carved rain-god masks, "a monumental prayer in stone."
- The Descent of the Ganges, Mahabalipuram (7th c. CE) — one of the largest rock reliefs on earth, reportedly once a working water-reenactment of its own myth.

**Strong**
- Tlaloc monolith, Mexico (largest monolithic sculpture in the Americas; rain broke out unseasonably the day it arrived in Mexico City in 1964).
- Fuxi and Nüwa / Gong Gong flood myth, Wu Liang Shrine reliefs, China (2nd c. CE) — among the oldest surviving figural art from China.
- Matsya, the fish avatar of Vishnu — a third, independent ancient "build a boat, save the seeds" flood tradition alongside Gilgamesh and Noah.
- Poseidon Ennosigaios ("earth-shaker") — earthquake-specific worship in seismic Greek regions like Corinth.
- Deucalion and Pyrrha — the Greek flood myth, structurally near-identical to Gilgamesh and Genesis.

**Solid**
- Chalchiuhtlicue, Aztec water/flood goddess (tied directly to the Sun Stone's Fourth Sun).
- Hapi and Sekhmet, Egypt (fertility-flood vs. plague-and-destruction, Karnak reliefs, Amenhotep III's hundreds of Sekhmet statues).
- Susanoo, Raijin, and Fujin, Japan (Sōtatsu's "Wind God and Thunder God" screen) — devotional rather than direct disaster-response, but a strong visual-culture entry.

**Strong**

- Hemingway's "Who Murdered the Vets?" (1935) — deepens the live South Florida hurricane-culture case, accountability rather than commemoration.

**Solid, or flagged as an intentional outlier**

## F. Disaster into recurring community event

**Exceptional**
- Qingming Water-Releasing Festival, Dujiangyan, China — continuous since 256 BCE, the oldest living example across all the research.
- Wafaa El-Nil, Egypt — ancient Hapi-worship lineage, still a two-week modern festival.
- Þjóðhátíð, Heimaey, Iceland — survived a literal volcanic burial of a third of the town in 1973 and kept going that same year.

**Strong**
- Kobe Luminarie, Japan (since 1995) — 200,000 lights as a deliberate electricity/recovery metaphor, 3–5 million visitors.
- Disaster Prevention Day, Japan (since 1960) — a whole country's civil-defense calendar built around one date (Sept 1, 1923 Kantō earthquake).
- Feast of San Gennaro's blood miracle, Naples — folk-religious Vesuvius-adjacent ritual, first recorded 1389.

**Solid**
- The Boston Christmas tree from Nova Scotia (since 1918, revived 1971) — gratitude rather than grief or warning, a genuinely different emotional register.
- Katrina second-line parades / "Katrina-versary," New Orleans — an existing ritual form (second line) repurposed onto a disaster anniversary rather than invented from scratch.

## G. Commemorative monuments, plaques, and markers

**Exceptional**
- Monument to the Great Fire of London (1677) — its 202-foot height literally encodes the distance to the fire's origin point; still climbable.
- Johnstown Flood National Memorial — the failure site (South Fork Dam remains) *is* the memorial, not a symbolic stand-in.

**Strong**
- Nilometers, Egypt (Rhoda Island example still standing, dated 861 CE) — infrastructure doubling as ritual object.
- Halifax Explosion Memorial Bell Tower — a single survivor's private donation becoming a permanent public monument decades later.
- Erwang Temple, China — the built-monument half of the Qingming festival entry above.

**Solid, or comparison-point only**
- The Mexico City "Monumentos NO reconstrucción" mural and the Whitney Torres-Ferrer piece — already in the tool; worth naming as the genre's opposite pole (protest against, or repurposing of, official memorial-making).

## H. High-water marks specifically

**Exceptional**
- Passau, Germany — flood marks on the Rathaus back to the 11th century; the single richest version of this idea found anywhere.
- Tsunami stones, Japan (Aneyoshi and others) — already live; see Category J below for the darker twist (some have been washed away by the very tsunamis they warned about).

**Strong**
- The Zouave of Pont de l'Alma, Paris — an accidental flood gauge since 1910.
- Baiheliang (White Crane Ridge), China — the inverse case, a drought marker rather than a flood one, tracking 72 low-water years across 1,000+ years.

**Open thread**
- A specific historic Ganges flood marker — plausible, widely assumed, but not confirmed despite searching; worth a targeted follow-up starting from a specific city and flood year (Varanasi, Patna, Prayagraj) rather than the river as a whole.

## I. Oral tradition as its own mechanism

**Exceptional**
- Smong, Simeulue Island — composed after the 1907 tsunami, credited with limiting 2004 deaths on the island to roughly three to five. The single strongest oral-tradition case in the whole research corpus, and it belongs folded directly into the existing live Simeulue case.
- Thunderbird and Whale, Cascadia — Pacific Northwest oral tradition cross-verified against Japanese written "orphan tsunami" records, independently dating the same 1700 earthquake from opposite sides of the Pacific.

**Strong**
- Gunditjmara songlines, Australia — narratives matching paleoclimatic sea-level data from 13,000–7,000 years ago.
- Griot oral tradition and Sahelian famine memory, West Africa — a formal, hereditary professional class functioning as a living archival institution; the 1740s–50s "Great Famine" survived roughly two centuries in oral form before being written down.

## J. Heritage destruction: a break in the narrative, not just forgetting

A genuinely distinct category from everything else, your newest thread and one of the strongest additions this round.

**Exceptional**
- Tsunami stones physically washed away by the tsunamis they warned about, Japan — the literal, non-metaphorical version of the whole idea; paired with the "three generations to lose disaster memory without a written record" finding.
- **[PAUSED — under review, not deleted]** The Kakhovka Dam, Ukraine (2023) — a "natural" disaster that was actually an act of war, drowning museums and Bronze Age/Scythian archaeological sites; the starkest possible conflict-plus-disaster overlap.
- Aleppo, Syria — the 2023 earthquake hitting a UNESCO site already war-damaged and already on the Heritage-in-Danger list since 2013; the clearest documented case of conflict directly compounding a natural disaster's destructive power.

**Strong**
- **[PAUSED — under review, not deleted]** The Timbuktu manuscripts, Mali (2012) — deliberate ideological destruction (Ansar Dine) versus an extraordinary, risk-everything rescue by local librarians who smuggled ~350,000 manuscripts out by donkey and boat.
- **[PAUSED — under review, not deleted]** Diego de Landa and the Maya codices (1562) — colonial religious destruction of a written tradition whose disaster/calendar content is now permanently unknowable; the destroyer is also, ironically, the main surviving source on what was destroyed.
- The 1923 Great Kantō earthquake and the massacre of Korean residents, Japan — a disaster that produced a real atrocity, which was then itself institutionally suppressed for decades; a break caused by silence rather than an object destroyed.

## K. Vernacular and disaster-resistant architecture (hazard-shaped design)

**Exceptional**
- Haiti's "gingerbread" houses, Port-au-Prince — only ~5% collapse rate in the 2010 earthquake versus ~40% citywide; a genuinely hard number.
- Hōryū-ji's five-story pagoda, Japan (607 CE) — flexible central-pillar joinery, only two comparable pagodas lost to earthquakes in 1,400 years, directly cited as an influence on modern seismic engineering.
- Omo sebua, Nias Island, Indonesia — earthquake-resistant chief's houses right next to the tool's existing Simeulue/Aceh material, tested again in the same 2004–2005 quakes.

**Strong**
- The bahay kubo / cinder-block status inversion, Philippines — a hazard-adapted vernacular tradition actively out-competed on status rather than suppressed or outlived; a genuinely distinct third mechanism for Degradation.
- Circular/domed cyclone housing, coastal India — aerodynamic shape as passive wind defense.
- Lombok, Indonesia's timber houses (2018 earthquakes) — a very recent, clean data point for wood-over-masonry.

## L. Vernacular architecture and historical rebuilding trends (the "before and after" version of K)

**Exceptional**
- Gaiola pombalina, Lisbon (developed immediately after 1755) — deepens the live Lisbon case directly; one of the first deliberate, city-scale earthquake-engineered urban plans anywhere.
- The Great Chicago Fire (1871) — codes rewritten within three years, a direct engineering lineage credited toward the skyscraper, with an honest caveat about who could and couldn't afford to rebuild fireproof.

**Strong**
- Terpen, Frisia/North Sea coast (500 BCE–1200 CE) — 17 centuries of mound-building, including a documented abandon-and-return cycle in the 3rd century CE; would substantially promote the already-dimmed Netherlands case.
- Fires in Edo, Japan (1600s–1868) — a 267-year rolling cycle of fire and incremental urban redesign (firebreaks, deconcentration), rather than one dated turning point.

## M. Participatory and incremental post-disaster housing ("half a good house")

**Exceptional**
- Quinta Monroy, Iquique, Chile (2004) — the original Elemental/Aravena project; poverty housing rather than strictly disaster recovery, but the origin of the whole idea.
- Constitución, Chile — Elemental's direct disaster-recovery sequel, after the 2010 earthquake/tsunami, pairing incremental housing with a tsunami-buffer forest instead of a seawall. Probably the single best candidate for an actual new case in this entire document if the "half a good house" thread becomes one.

**Strong**
- Yasmeen Lari's Barefoot Social Architecture, Pakistan (2010/2022 floods) — self-build bamboo shelters, "communities as partners, not victims," a mirror-image version of the same underlying wager.

## N. Fire and land management as ritual

**Strong, and best read as a set, not individually ranked**
- Northern Australia (already live) — the anchor.
- Karuk, Yurok, and Hoopa tribes, Northern California — "good fire," pest control via burning, a near-identical suppress-then-reinvite arc to Australia's.
- Krahô and Xavante peoples, Brazilian Cerrado — ceremonial group hunts that are simultaneously the land-management act itself; Brazil's own suppress-then-reinvite policy reversal in 2014.
- Cross-reference to water-ritual equivalents already elsewhere in this document: Dujiangyan's festival, Wafaa El-Nil.

## O. Institutional and celebrity-led preparedness campaigns

**Exceptional**

**Strong**
- Smokey Bear, USA (since 1944) — the obvious historical ancestor; longest-running PSA campaign in American history.
- Cyclone Preparedness Programme, Bangladesh (since 1973) — the volunteer-network, non-media-campaign version of the same goal; 76,000+ trained volunteers.

**Open thread**
- A sports-celebrity-fronted preparedness campaign outside the Caribbean — searched for specifically, not found; worth another pass starting from a specific national disaster-management agency rather than the sport or celebrity angle.

## P. Children and disaster: creative-arts, education, and trauma care

**Strong, best read as a set (the original creative-arts pass)**
- The Flying Seagull Project (founded 2008) — circus/play in refugee camps and disaster zones, 130,000+ children reached.
- Za'atari refugee camp, Jordan — Make Life Skate Life's skateboarding program plus the Makani centers.
- Already-live cross-references worth keeping tied to these: the Peregian and creative-arts (New Orleans) cases.

**Exceptional — the education/prevention side (new)**
- Tsunami tendenko and the "Miracle of Kamaishi," Japan (curriculum from 2005, tested 2011) — 99.8% child survival rate, directly credited to the training rather than luck; arguably the single best-evidenced children's disaster-education case anywhere, and a natural companion to the smong material in Category I.

**Strong — institutional and clinical models (new)**
- Child Friendly Spaces, Save the Children / UNICEF — the standardized humanitarian model underneath most disaster-zone children's programs globally.
- Kokoro no kea, Japan (built after Kobe 1995, reactivated after Tōhoku 2011) — a school-embedded psychosocial care system, the clearest institutional link between "teaching what happened" and "helping them process it."
- Ahlan Simsim, Sesame Workshop/IRC (since 2020) — Muppet-based trauma content for displaced children, redeployed repeatedly across the Morocco/Turkey-Syria earthquakes and Libya floods; has actual outcomes research behind it.
- Chernobyl Children International's Rest and Recuperation Programme, Ireland (since 1991) — a genuinely different mechanism, physical removal from ongoing contamination rather than talk or play, 25,500+ children hosted.

*A four-part taxonomy suggested by this expanded set: informal creative outlet, structured school curriculum, mass-media/parasocial intervention, and physical removal from ongoing harm — four different answers to what a child needs after a disaster. Full detail in its own document.*

## S. Hurricane parties and the climate-change wrinkle

Now grounded in the actual Eater article (Hannah C. Griggs, "Storm's Coming. Send Out the Invites.," 2019), which Daniel provided directly after the site came back blocked in this environment.

**Exceptional**
- The privilege/equity thread: a 1969 Philadelphia Tribune quote drawn directly from the article draws a hard line between "hurricane parties" (per the Tribune, "for wealthy white folk") and the "praying parties" held by poorer Black Mississippi Coast residents after Hurricane Camille destroyed their homes. Griggs's own line, "the ability to be vulnerable and emerge unscathed is an immense privilege," is sharp enough to deserve its own section rather than folding quietly into the existing hurricane-culture case.
- The 1962 interracial hurricane party (via a 1981 New York Amsterdam News retrospective) — a storm briefly suspending Jim Crow-era policing enough for students, faculty, and musicians to gather across race lines with "no fear of police raids." A genuinely surprising, well-sourced angle.

**Strong**
- The tradition's real origin story (pre-WWII, post-Prohibition, the "one-quart/two-quart/three-quart" storm-grading convention) — deepens the live South Florida hurricane-culture case directly.
- The Richelieu Manor/Hurricane Camille (1969) cautionary legend — disputed veracity, but real evidence of a folk story doing actual disaster-risk-communication work regardless of whether it happened as told.
- The rapid-intensification argument (a separate, still-standing addition from the first research pass, not from the article itself): storms now outrunning the lead time the hurricane-party tradition assumes, a clean echo of the tool's Formulation-to-Degradation arc.

## T. From Lucy Jones's *The Big Ones* and Solnit's *A Paradise Built in Hell*

- **Exceptional:** the 1927 Mississippi flood's civil-rights dimension (Jones) — forced Black labor at gunpoint, credited as a factor in the Great Migration; deepens the already-live levee-breaks case rather than requiring a new one.
- **Strong:** Iceland's 1783 Laki eruption (Jones, also listed under Category C); the 1906 San Francisco mutual-aid story and the 1917 Halifax Explosion (Solnit, both also listed under Category C/B).

## U. Deliberate testimony archives (the "community's own Yad Vashem" thread)

Distinct from Category I: not memory that survives informally (song, oral tradition), but a conscious, resourced decision to systematically collect survivor/elder testimony into a real repository.

**Exceptional**
- Kataribe, Kobe (since 1995) — 150+ trained survivor-storytellers still giving firsthand accounts three decades on; the closest single match to the Yad Vashem model in spirit.
- The 3.11 Densho Road network, Tōhoku (built out post-2011) — 300+ connected memorial sites with named survivor guides; would substantially strengthen the dimmed Tohoku case (see Category B).
- UC QuakeStudies / CEISMIC, Christchurch (since 2012) — 55,000+ items, 700+ recorded QuakeBox testimonies in 13 languages, plus a nested, volunteer-run Women's Voices sub-archive.

**Strong**
- Voices from the Dust Bowl, Library of Congress (recorded 1940–41) — the oldest example, and a different authorship model (outside folklorists, not the affected community itself).
- Voices from the Storm, New Orleans (2008, Voice of Witness) — published book-form testimony later re-adapted into a live theater script by high schoolers, a rare case of testimony being deliberately re-transmitted through a second medium.
- The Aceh Tsunami Museum's Survivor Stories collection (opened 2009) — deepens the already-live Aceh case directly, testimony placed intentionally beside the hazard science rather than replacing it.

*Full detail, including the different collection models (survivor-led vs. researcher-led vs. institution-trained volunteers), in its own document.*

## V. Transmitting a living culture through ongoing climate displacement

Distinct from everything above: not a culture remembering a disaster that already happened, but a culture trying to carry itself forward through a slow-motion one that hasn't finished yet.

**Exceptional**
- Wokbaot Wetem Kalja ("moving with culture"), Vanuatu (IOM project, 2021–2024) — built from the real Ambae double-evacuation case; directly shaped Vanuatu's 2018 national displacement policy to formally protect custom and traditional knowledge through relocation. The cleanest match for "helping transmit a living culture since the country is disappearing."
- Kwajalein Atoll Sustainability Laboratory (KASL), Marshall Islands (founded 2022) — Marshallese-run, youth-education-focused climate adaptation lab; would substantially deepen the already-dimmed Marshall Islands case from a purely historical entry into a present-tense one.

**Strong**
- The ahupua'a system, Hawaii (precolonial, active revival via KUA and others) — reviving a full mountain-to-sea land-management system as a genuine climate-resilience candidate, not just heritage; ties directly to the live Maui/Lahaina case's pre-plantation water-system material.
- Isle de Jean Charles, Louisiana (resettlement completed 2022) — the first federally funded US climate relocation, included specifically for its honest, unresolved sting: a real dispute over who counts as "in" the tribe once the community has been physically broken apart, worth keeping as a cautionary counterweight to the other three.

*Full detail in its own document.*

## A rough sense of where the real density is

If this needs to collapse into fewer, more intuitive buckets later rather than staying as twenty research-pass categories, the material seems to cluster into a few natural super-groups worth considering: (1) built and physical memory — monuments, markers, architecture, both the hazard-resistant kind and the historically-adapted kind; (2) transmitted and performed memory — oral tradition, festivals, music, literature, visual art; (3) institutional memory — preparedness campaigns, infrastructure, policy responses, both the kind that work and the kind that get suppressed; (4) broken memory — heritage destruction, forgetting, commodification, the tool's existing Degradation material plus this round's darker additions; and (5) community repair — the participatory-housing and creative-arts-therapy material, which is really about the process of recovering rather than the memory of the disaster itself. Worth testing that five-part structure against whatever survives your pruning pass.
