What breaks when a storm like Ian hits?
Ian produced two nearly non-overlapping disasters from one storm. This tab maps the first — a slow-motion insurance-market collapse concentrated on the coast — as its own scored layer on the Adaptive Capacity axis, not blended into the vulnerability score above. The second track — undocumented-farmworker exclusion from FEMA aid, concentrated in Immokalee — is scored as a Vulnerability dimension (see that tab); it's the same mechanism already built for the Western NC tool, reused here rather than reinvented.

272,299 insurance claims were filed in Lee County alone from Ian — more than a third of every claim filed statewide. Six FL insurers went insolvent in 2022; Citizens Property Insurance (the state backstop) swelled past 1.1M policies. Fort Myers Beach premiums nearly doubled, 2019–2024.
Resilience
What is already working?
Babcock Ranch (Charlotte County, just north of the Lee County line) had zero loss of power, water, or internet during Ian, built on underground utility lines, engineered stormwater wetlands, and above-code elevation — while >90% of surrounding Charlotte County lost power. It's shown as a reference marker, not part of the scored Lee/Collier grid. Caveat: homes there run $300K–$1M+, and insurers still give no discount for the demonstrated resilience — worth holding alongside the "just build it like this" temptation.

Human Stories
What did it feel like to live it?

The Fort Myers Beach retiree and the 50% rule

An insurer goes insolvent, the replacement premium nearly doubles, and now the choice is a full elevation-compliant rebuild or losing the property — legally entitled to every recovery program that exists, undone by the market itself.

The San Carlos Island shrimper

Most of the ~40-boat fleet was thrown into the mangroves. Three years on, only one property's docks remain from a waterfront that once housed Florida's largest shrimp fleet.

The Immokalee farmworker FEMA can't see

Same storm as the one making national news 30 miles away — never appears in a FEMA registration count because applying risked exposing undocumented status. RCMA and the Farmworker Association of Florida filled the gap instead.

Poinsettia vs. Lazy J, 200 yards apart

Two mobile home parks across Ortiz Avenue from each other in Fort Myers — one devastated, one barely touched. The concrete argument for why hex-grid resolution matters more than a county or zip average.

Cape Coral: sold as paradise, covered as an afterthought

Built by a 1950s–60s land-sales operation flying buyers in for free steak dinners, mostly from the Midwest and Northeast. 99% of the city lost power, but coverage skewed to the more photogenic destruction at Fort Myers Beach and Sanibel.

The assisted-living resident who depended on power

One of Lee County's Ian deaths (72 by the county's own tally; other credible counts run lower) tied to an electric medical device losing power countywide — adaptive capacity as infrastructure dependency, not documentation status or insurance affordability.
